The Urine Test Strip Reader is a point-of-care diagnostic system designed to automatically analyze urine test strips using controlled illumination, computer vision, and AI-based concentration prediction. Instead of relying on subjective visual comparison with a printed colour chart, the system captures and processes the colour changes of all 10 reagent pads to generate structured numerical results.

Medical Disclaimer: The Urine Test Strip Reader is intended to support screening and clinical decision-making. Results must be reviewed and interpreted by a qualified clinician or pathologist before any diagnosis or treatment decision is made.



Diagnostic Process
  • Sample Preparation: A urine test strip is dipped into the patient's urine sample and placed inside the device holder. A biochemical reaction begins across all 10 reagent pads.
  • Timed Reaction: The system waits for 120 seconds to allow the colour reactions to develop. During this period, no image is captured.
  • Controlled Image Capture: After the reaction period, a Raspberry Pi camera automatically captures an image of the strip inside a sealed black-box enclosure. Brightness-regulated LED illumination provides uniform and calibrated lighting, helping reduce errors caused by ambient light.
  • Computer Vision Analysis: An OpenCV-based computer vision pipeline isolates each of the 10 reagent pads and calculates the average RGB colour values for every pad.
  • AI-Based Concentration Prediction: The extracted RGB information is processed by a trained non-linear regression model. The model maps the colour data to continuous numerical concentrations for the different urine parameters.
  • Digital Report Generation: The system generates a structured digital report containing the patient ID, timestamp, and predicted analyte concentrations. Results can then be securely stored and transmitted to a hospital-compatible database.
  • Clinical Review: Abnormal or out-of-range results are highlighted for clinical attention. A qualified clinician or pathologist reviews the findings before any clinical action is taken.

Important: The device output should be used as clinical decision support and should not replace professional medical judgement or confirmatory diagnostic testing where required.

Important Test Parameters (10 Key Parameters)

Glucose: Reported in mg/dL. Elevated readings should be reviewed in the appropriate clinical context.

Protein: Reported in mg/dL and used as an important parameter during routine urinalysis.

pH (Potential of Hydrogen): Indicates the acidity or alkalinity of urine.

Ketones: Reported in mg/dL and used as part of metabolic assessment.

Blood / RBC: Indicates the detection of blood-related components in the urine sample.

Bilirubin: An important parameter that may contribute to liver-related clinical assessment.

Urobilinogen: Supports the interpretation of liver and metabolic processes.

Nitrite: A clinically relevant parameter that may be considered when assessing possible UTI.

Leucocytes / WBC: May indicate the presence of white blood cells and should be interpreted by a healthcare professional.

Specific Gravity / SG: Indicates the concentration of urine.

Healthcare Instructions
  • Use the appropriate urine test strip and follow the manufacturer's sample-handling instructions.
  • Allow the complete reaction time before image analysis.
  • Ensure the strip is correctly positioned inside the device before image capture.
  • Review all abnormal or flagged results through a qualified clinician or pathologist.
  • Do not use a single test result as an independent diagnosis.
  • Maintain patient privacy and data security when storing or transmitting patient-linked test results.
  • Use the system as a decision-support tool, with final clinical interpretation remaining with the healthcare professional.

Warning: An abnormal result does not independently confirm a disease or medical condition, and a normal result does not rule out all possible conditions. Further clinical evaluation or confirmatory testing may be required.
